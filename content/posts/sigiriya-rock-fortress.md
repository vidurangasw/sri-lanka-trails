Rising 200 metres above the surrounding jungle, **Sigiriya Rock Fortress** is arguably Sri Lanka's most dramatic and awe-inspiring sight. Carved into a volcanic plug of ancient lava, this 5th-century rock citadel was built by King Kashyapa and is today one of the best-preserved examples of ancient urban planning in the world.

## Why Visit Sigiriya?

Sigiriya is not just a rock — it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, one of the Eight Wonders of Asia, and a masterpiece of ancient engineering. The site includes:

- **The Lion's Paw Terrace** — massive carved lion's paws that once flanked the stairway to the summit
- **The Mirror Wall** — a polished surface so reflective that the king could see himself as he walked past
- **The Sigiriya Frescoes** — vivid 5th-century paintings of celestial maidens, considered some of the finest ancient paintings in Asia
- **The Summit Palace** — royal gardens and pools at the very top with panoramic views across the jungle

## Getting There

Sigiriya is located in the **Cultural Triangle** of Sri Lanka's North Central Province, approximately:

- **4 hours** from Colombo by car
- **3 hours** from Kandy by car
- **30 minutes** from Dambulla

The nearest town is **Dambulla**, which has good accommodation options.

## Best Time to Visit

Climb early — very early. The rock becomes significantly hotter as the day progresses, and midday heat can make the steep climb genuinely exhausting.

| Time | Experience |
|------|------------|
| **6:00 AM – 8:00 AM** | Cool, magical light, small crowds |
| **8:00 AM – 10:00 AM** | Warm but manageable |
| **10:00 AM – 3:00 PM** | Hot, crowded, tiring |
| **3:00 PM – 5:30 PM** | Good light returns, pleasant |

> **Pro Tip:** Arrive at opening time (7:00 AM) to beat both the heat and the tour groups. The sunrise view from the top is extraordinary.

## Entrance Fees (2026)

- **Foreign Adults:** USD $30 (or equivalent in LKR)
- **Foreign Children (6–12):** USD $15
- **SAARC Nationals:** Discounted rates apply
- **Sri Lankan Citizens:** Much lower rates apply

Tickets can be purchased at the gate. There is no online booking system at the time of writing, so arrive early during peak season.

## The Climb: What to Expect

The total climb involves approximately **1,200 steps** in various sections. Here is what you will encounter:

1. **Water Gardens** — Beautiful symmetrical gardens at the base. Worth exploring before you begin the ascent.
2. **Boulder Gardens** — Massive boulders with caves that once housed monks.
3. **Terraced Gardens** — Ancient terracing carved into the rock face.
4. **Lion's Paw Terrace** — The iconic twin lion's paws. The original lion's head has collapsed but the base remains.
5. **Final Staircase** — A series of metal staircases bolted to the sheer rock face. Slightly vertiginous but very safe.
6. **The Summit** — Ruins of the palace and gardens, with 360° views. Priceless.

## Practical Tips

- **Wear comfortable shoes** with grip. Flip-flops are not recommended.
- **Bring water** — there are vendors at the base but not at the top.
- **Respect the frescoes** — Photography is not permitted on the frescoes level (you can photograph the view but not the paintings themselves).
- **Wasps** — The rock has some wasp nests. Move quietly and do not make sudden movements near the hives.
- **Combine with Pidurangala** — The nearby Pidurangala Rock offers a stunning *free* view of Sigiriya from the side, often considered the better photograph.

## Where to Stay Near Sigiriya

- **Aliya Resort & Spa** — Luxury with elephant views from the pool
- **Water Garden Sigiriya** — Boutique luxury in the paddy fields
- **Sigiriya Village Hotel** — Mid-range, great for families
- **Local homestays** — Budget-friendly and culturally immersive
